# MCA vs MACC — What's the Difference?

## Quick Summary

| Term | Full Name | What It Is |
|------|-----------|------------|
| **MCA** | Microsoft Customer Agreement | A **contract** — how you buy from Microsoft |
| **MACC** | Microsoft Azure Consumption Commitment | A **spending promise** — how much you agree to spend on Azure |

---

## MCA — Microsoft Customer Agreement

An **MCA** is the legal agreement (contract) you sign with Microsoft to use their products and services. Think of it as the **membership form** you fill out when joining a service.

- It replaced older, more complex agreements (like the Enterprise Agreement) with a simpler, digital contract.
- Once you have an MCA, you can buy and use Microsoft products like Azure, Microsoft 365, and more.
- It covers the **terms and conditions** of your relationship with Microsoft.

**In one line:** MCA = *the contract that governs how you use Microsoft services.*

---

## MACC — Microsoft Azure Consumption Commitment

A **MACC** is a promise to spend a specific amount of money on Azure over a set period (usually 1–3 years). In return, Microsoft may offer discounts or other benefits.

- It is **not** a separate contract — it lives *inside* an existing agreement (like an MCA or an Enterprise Agreement).
- You commit up-front to consume a certain dollar amount of Azure services.
- If you don't reach your committed spend, you may still owe the committed amount.

**In one line:** MACC = *a cloud spending commitment you make as part of a larger deal.*

---

## Side-by-Side Comparison

| | **MCA** | **MACC** |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | A contract / agreement | A spending commitment |
| **Purpose** | Sets the terms of your Microsoft relationship | Locks in a minimum Azure spend for benefits |
| **Required?** | Yes — you need a contract to buy services | Optional — only if you want volume discounts |
| **Standalone?** | Yes — can exist on its own | No — sits inside an MCA or similar agreement |
| **Duration** | Ongoing (no fixed end date by default) | Fixed term (e.g., 1, 2, or 3 years) |
| **Involves money?** | Not directly | Yes — a committed dollar amount |

---

## Example Scenario

> **Contoso Ltd.** is a mid-sized company moving to the cloud.

1. **Step 1 — Sign the MCA:** Contoso signs a Microsoft Customer Agreement. This gives them the legal right to purchase and use Microsoft cloud services. No money is committed yet — it's just the contract.

2. **Step 2 — Add a MACC:** Because Contoso plans to use a lot of Azure, they negotiate a **MACC of $200,000 over 2 years**. In exchange, Microsoft gives them a discount on Azure services.

3. **Result:** Contoso now has:
   - An **MCA** → the contract that governs everything.
   - A **MACC** → the promise to spend $200,000 on Azure within 2 years.

The MCA would still exist even without the MACC. The MACC is an *add-on commitment* made under the MCA.

---

## In One Sentence

> **MCA** is *how* you buy from Microsoft; **MACC** is *how much* you promise to spend on Azure.
