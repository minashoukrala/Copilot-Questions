# Can a Company Have EA, MACC, ACD, and MCA All Together?

**Short answer: Yes — and it is more common than you might think.**

---

## What Do These Terms Mean?

| Term | Full Name | What It Is (Simply Put) |
|------|-----------|------------------------|
| **EA** | Enterprise Agreement | The *contract* a large company signs with Microsoft, usually for 3 years, covering software licenses and cloud services. Think of it as the **membership plan**. |
| **MACC** | Microsoft Azure Consumption Commitment | A promise the company makes to *spend* a minimum amount on Azure over a set period. Think of it as the **minimum spend pledge** inside a deal. |
| **ACD** | Azure Credit Discount | A *discount* applied to Azure services in return for that commitment. Think of it as the **reward** the company gets for making the MACC pledge. |
| **MCA** | Microsoft Customer Agreement | A newer, simpler, all-digital contract for buying Microsoft cloud products. Think of it as the **modern, paperless contract**. |

---

## How Can All Four Exist at the Same Time?

Each term plays a **different role**, so they do not conflict:

1. **EA** → the overall contract/agreement  
2. **MACC** → a spending commitment added *on top of* the EA (or large MCA)  
3. **ACD** → a discount automatically applied *because of* the MACC  
4. **MCA** → a separate or parallel contract, often used for newer subscriptions or different departments  

---

## A Simple Real-World Scenario

> **Contoso Ltd.** is a large company that has been a Microsoft customer for years.
>
> - They have an **EA** signed 2 years ago for their main software licenses and Azure environment.
> - As part of that EA, they committed to spend **$1,000,000 on Azure** — this is their **MACC**.
> - Because of that commitment, Microsoft gives them an **ACD** (a discount on Azure services as a reward for the pledge).
> - Meanwhile, a newly acquired subsidiary of Contoso signs a fresh **MCA** for its own Microsoft 365 and Azure subscriptions, because MCA is the modern, simpler option for new deals.
>
> **Result:** Contoso Ltd. is now operating with all four — EA, MACC, ACD, and MCA — at the same time, each serving a different purpose.

## Summary Table

| | EA | MACC | ACD | MCA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | Contract type | Spend commitment | Discount | Contract type |
| **Who sets it** | Microsoft + Company | Company pledges | Microsoft grants | Microsoft + Company |
| **Can it coexist with the others?** | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes |
| **Required for the others?** | Often the base | Needs EA or large MCA | Triggered by MACC | Standalone or alongside EA |

---

## Key Takeaway

> **EA and MCA are both contract types** (you can have one per business unit or transition between them).  
> **MACC is a commitment** layered on top of either.  
> **ACD is a benefit** earned automatically when a MACC is in place.  
>
> Together, they describe *how* a company buys, *how much* it commits to spending, and *what discounts* it earns — all at once.
